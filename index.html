Working Papers

  • Markdown Pricing with Strategic Customers and Reference Effects
    with N. Bora Keskin and Jing-Sheng Song
    Presented at: Behavioral Operations Conference Emergence Research Workshop (AZ, May 2026)
    Scheduled presentations: INFORMS 2026, San Francisco — session “Learning and Information Design in Pricing,” Tuesday, November 3, 1:15–2:30 PM, Moscone South-306
    Abstract

    We study a two-period markdown pricing model in which a fraction of customers strategically time their purchases and the remainder evaluate the markdown against the first-period price. The subgame-perfect equilibrium partitions the parameter space into two pricing regimes, separated by a threshold on market patience. In the population-pooling regime, the seller sells to both customer types in both periods and manages the depth of the markdown. In the strategic-deferral regime, the seller concedes period-1 sales to reference customers and sets the first-period price primarily to anchor the period-2 reference, monetizing the full market at the markdown. The two-regime mechanism is robust to three extensions of the baseline model: uncertainty about the strategic fraction, a four-segment customer population that introduces a hybrid-deferral regime, and exponential-smoothing of the reference price. Calibrated counterfactuals show that ignoring customer heterogeneity costs up to 7% of optimal revenue under pooling and up to 40% under deferral, while recognizing reference-price effects adds up to 1% under pooling and up to 7% under deferral.

  • The Value of AI in Markdown Pricing
    with N. Bora Keskin and Jing-Sheng Song
    Abstract

    We study two-period markdown pricing when artificial intelligence enters on both sides of the market. On the customer side, shoppers may adopt an AI purchasing agent that uses price history to time purchases optimally; adoption is endogenous, so the strategic fraction of the market is an equilibrium object rather than a primitive. We show that the value of the agent equals the timing-option gain and is proportional to the square of the equilibrium markdown. Because the equilibrium markdown shrinks as adoption rises, AI adoption is self-limiting: there is a unique interior adoption equilibrium whenever agents are costly, and cheaper agents compress the very deals they are built to capture. On the seller side, the market scale is unknown and the seller’s AI produces a signal of accuracy ρ. Period-1 demand identifies the scale exactly in equilibrium, so the continuation price never depends on the signal; the seller’s AI is purely a first-period instrument, and its value is increasing and convex in accuracy. The two results share one message: on both sides of the market, AI creates value only through the first-period price, and equilibrium responses cap how much value it can create.
